What other trip platforms show on their dashboard

Research, 3 August 2026 · for the Swellyo operator dashboard

First, a correction. There is no competitor comparison anywhere in our docs. I searched all of docs/. The only outside product we ever wrote about is Klook, in the promotion spec. So this is fresh research, not a re-read.

I looked at four platforms that do what we want to do, plus the general tour-operator booking tools. Below: what each one shows, then the pattern they all share, then what it means for us.

The four platforms

TrovaTrip Creators take their audience on trips. Closest to us. most useful

Their "Host Portal" has three tabs. The interesting one is Audience — nobody else has it.

  • Home — what needs your attention right now, and where each trip is in the process.
  • Audience — survey answers from their followers, live: top destinations asked for, travel budget, age, gender. This is how a host decides where to run the next trip.
  • Bookings — traveler list with booking status, how much each one paid, and traveler details.
  • Money shown as potential earnings, live, not just money already in.
  • Bookings are grouped by status: pending, approved, waitlisted.
  • The host approves or rejects each booking by hand.
  • Important dates and deadlines have their own page.
Takeaway: they treat "who wants to come" as data, not just a list.
WeTravel Booking + payments for multi-day trips. The most complete back office.
  • Sales graph — value of packages and add-ons sold so far, paid and unpaid, minus discounts. Cancelled bookings do not count.
  • Number of travelers booked in a chosen period, minus cancellations.
  • Number of people on the waitlist.
  • A cash-flow report: money collected, net collected, money still owed.
  • One reporting screen across all trips, not per trip — to spot trends.
  • Every number exports to CSV, XLSX or PNG.
  • Participant manifest — traveler info collected automatically, ready to send to a hotel or partner in one click.
  • Documents (passports, waivers, dietary) are collected and managed from the dashboard.
  • Bulk actions — change payment plans or prices for many bookings at once.
  • Message all travelers, or one, and schedule the message for later.
  • Auto-reminders for tasks that are not done.
Takeaway: the export button is everywhere, and reminders are automatic.
YouLi Group travel operations. Closest to our documents-and-tasks idea.
  • Everything is a task: forms, waivers, profile, packing list, passport. One model for all of it — same as our requirements.
  • Tasks go both ways: things you need from the traveler, and things you must make sure they received.
  • Each traveler gets their own portal: their tasks, their payments, their waivers, the itinerary.
  • Traveler sees what is due next — next payment and next unfinished task.
  • Planner side = one dashboard for the whole trip.
  • Automated reminders at set intervals, for tasks and for invoices.
  • Manifests update themselves when something changes — the pitch is "not a spreadsheet".
Takeaway: our requirements model is the right shape. They just push harder on reminders.
JoinMyTrip Solo travelers join a trip led by a "TripLeader". Mobile-first. thinnest
  • Who wants to join — the request list is the main screen.
  • All chats in one place, with a notification so the leader never misses a message.
  • Payments in and out, held in the app.
  • Deliberately mobile only. No desktop back office at all.
They are the least like a dashboard. Their bet: a trip leader is a person with a phone, not a company with a desk. Worth remembering — that is our mobile side.

The classic tour-operator tools

Bokun, Rezdy, Checkfront, FareHarbor, Xola. These sell day tours, not our kind of trip, so most of it does not apply. What they all show:

  • Revenue, by channel.
  • Occupancy — how full each departure is.
  • Availability in real time, synced across sales channels.
  • Payments with deposits and instalments.
  • Guide and resource scheduling.

Occupancy is the one worth stealing. Everything else here is for a business selling 40 tours a week.

The pattern — every one of them shows these

  • How full is the trip. Spots sold out of spots available.
  • How much money is in, how much is still owed. Never just one number.
  • What is waiting for me. A short list on the first screen. Approvals, unread, overdue.
  • Who is coming, in one list, with each person's status on the same row.
  • Every list exports. Because a hotel or a bus company always asks for a file.
  • Automatic reminders to travelers who have not finished something.
  • Deadlines shown as their own thing, not buried in the trip settings.

Traveler information they collect

This is the "manifest" — the file an operator hands to a hotel, an airline or a bus company.

  • Name and email
  • Passport details we have
  • Payment status not yet
  • Dietary needs and allergies we have
  • Emergency contact — name, email, and up to three phone numbers we do not have
  • Flight details, arrival and departure we do not have
  • Rooming — who shares with who we do not have
  • Waiver signed, yes or no we have
  • Insurance we have
One rule everyone follows: passports must not expire within six months of the trip. We store the expiry date but do not warn about it.

What I would take from this

1. Emergency contact and flight details. Every serious platform collects both. We collect neither. For a surf trip in El Salvador, an emergency contact is not a nice-to-have.
2. Automatic reminders. WeTravel and YouLi both send them without the operator doing anything. Today our operator has to chase people by hand. This is probably the highest value thing on the page.
3. Passport expiry warning. Six months. We already have the date. It is a comparison and a red badge.
4. "Spots sold" on the trip card. Everyone leads with it. We show days to go but not how full the trip is.
5. One export, whole trip. We export per traveler and per requirement. Nobody else does it that way — they export the whole trip as one manifest file, because that is what a hotel asks for.
6. The TrovaTrip audience idea — worth a real conversation with Eyal. They show a host what their followers want before the trip is even planned: top destinations, budgets, ages. We already hold all of that in surfer profiles and onboarding answers. Nobody else in this list can do it. It is not a dashboard feature, it is a reason for an operator to choose us.
7. What to skip. Channel revenue, OTA sync, guide scheduling, dynamic pricing. That is day-tour software for a company running 40 departures a week. Not us, not soon.
