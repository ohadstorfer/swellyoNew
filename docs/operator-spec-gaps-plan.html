Before anything is built

Five decisions

Each of these is a place where a past decision, written down and reasoned about at the time, says the opposite of the spec. Someone has to choose. A recommendation is given for each, but the recommendation is not the decision.

D1

Is an emergency contact medical data, or profile data?

On 24 August the medical card was narrowed to the operator of record alone, and the migration says why in as many words: "allergies, medication, injuries and an emergency contact are a medical record; the person who owns the trip is the only one who needs it." But two live strings promise the opposite — the app labels travelers.view_profiles as "Traveler profiles + emergency contact" (TripStaffSheet.tsx:79), and the site's own spec gives a Manager that row with a tick. Both cannot be true, and the field does not exist yet, so nothing has broken — it is about to.

The counter-argument is worth stating: the person who calls a traveler's family is the guide standing on the beach, not the owner who may be in another country.

Recommended Ship it under medical.view, matching the August ruling — it needs no new policy and no new capability — and fix the two stale strings so nobody is promised a field they cannot read. If the beach argument wins later, it becomes a narrow emergency.view capability, which is one row in organized_trip_staff_roles and no client release.

D2

May a Manager change the trip's refund terms?

The spec lets a Manager "edit anything besides dates & destinations", and the cancellation policy sits on group_trips, whose UPDATE policy is trip.edit — which a Manager holds. So on today's rules, a Manager could rewrite what everyone gets back.

Money is already fenced off from Managers everywhere else: prices authorise on group_trips.host_id, refunds need money.manage. Refund terms are money.

Recommended Owner only, guarded in the database the same way dates are — a trigger, because RLS cannot exclude two columns from a row. Same shape as trg_guard_operator_trip_dates.

D3

Who owns a crew member's description?

The spec says "Manage self — edit your own description". The code says the opposite, on purpose: "an account-holding guide's job and blurb belong to the OPERATOR, not to them. They are how this trip introduces its crew" (tripStaffService.ts:311). RLS agrees — every write to organized_trip_staff needs staff.manage.

Recommended Both, and they do not conflict: the operator keeps the right to edit anyone, and a crew member may edit their own title and bio and nothing else. It needs a column guard as well as a policy — an RLS policy scoped to user_id = auth.uid() would also let a guide promote themselves to co-operator.

D4

Does whole-trip document export ship on the phone?

"Export all docs" means every traveler's every file — on a busy trip, sixty private PDFs and photos. The site already builds that zip in the browser. The app has no zip at all: it shares one file at a time through the OS sheet.

Building it on mobile means holding the whole archive in JavaScript memory before writing it, on a device that will kill the app for less.

Recommended Per-traveler and per-requirement export ship on both. Whole-trip export stays on the desktop, and the app says so in a line rather than staying silent. This is the one place the "both surfaces" rule should bend, and it should bend out loud.

D5

Does chat come to the desktop?

The spec lists "chat" in every dashboard column. On the site it is the single largest item here by a wide margin: a realtime socket, the conversation list, attachments, read state, typing — a second messaging client, not a page.

Recommended Not in this pass. Ship admin updates on the site first (one table, no realtime, and it is the broadcast half of what an operator actually uses chat for), then decide whether the 1:1 half earns a desktop build on its own evidence.

Part one

The eight that exist nowhere

Spec'd, and not built on either surface. Four need a migration; four are presentation over reads that already run.

G1

Emergency contact on the medical form

App + Site Migration Blocked by D1 Small
Spec

Complete medical statement — injuries, allergies and dietary preferences, prescribed medicine, emergency contact.

Today

Four of the five. organized_trip_medical_forms holds allergies, dietary, injuries and medications and nothing else (20260724000200_operator_evidence_tables.sql:158); MedicalFormSheet.tsx:40 renders the same four.

Plan
  1. Migration. Three nullable columns on the medical form, plus their own length CHECK — the existing medical_text_len stays untouched rather than being dropped and rebuilt. 
    alter table public.organized_trip_medical_forms
  add column if not exists emergency_name     text,
  add column if not exists emergency_phone    text,
  add column if not exists emergency_relation text;

alter table public.organized_trip_medical_forms
  add constraint medical_emergency_len check (
    coalesce(length(emergency_name), 0)     <= 120 and
    coalesce(length(emergency_phone), 0)    <= 40  and
    coalesce(length(emergency_relation), 0) <= 60
  );
    No RLS change under D1's recommendation: the row is already medical.view, and these ride on it.
  2. App. A fifth block in MedicalFormSheet — name, phone, relationship. It is the one part of the form with no "none" escape, so the sheet's completeness rule has to learn that a contact is required where the other four accept "no allergies".
  3. Both dashboards. Show it beside the medical answers — TravelerExtras's Medical block and the site's TravelerPage medical card. A phone number that cannot be tapped to call is a phone number nobody uses: make it a tel: link on both.
  4. Copy fix. Correct CAPABILITY_LABELS['travelers.view_profiles'] and the Manager row in the site's SPEC.md, which both promise this field to people D1 does not give it to.
Done when

A traveler cannot finish onboarding without naming someone to call, and the operator can reach that person in two taps from the traveler's page — on either surface.

G2

The payments page

App + Site No database work Medium
Spec

Payments page — view all transactions, amounts, profiles, times, export options.

Today

Neither surface has a ledger. The site's Money page is per-traveler pricing and step state; the app's Money card is one collected-of-expected figure. Per-traveler history exists inside TravelerExtras and nowhere above it.

Plan

The data is already in hand on both surfaces and costs no extra round trip: TripMoney.events carries every counted event for the whole trip, newest first, in both operatorDashboardService.ts and the site's domain/money.ts. This is a page, not a feature.

  1. One row per event — date and time, traveler with their photo, which step it paid, amount, and the Stripe mode it belongs to. Refunds are stored negative, so they read as refunds without a second query.
  2. Show failed attempts, behind a toggle, off by default. They are filtered out of every existing total on purpose, and they are exactly what an operator wants when a traveler says "I paid and it didn't work".
  3. Keep the live/test line. Both apps already count only events matching the current mode and know how many they hid — say so on this page rather than silently dropping rows.
  4. Export. CSV, not a zip. The site reuses saveBlob; the app writes the file to cache and hands it to the share sheet, the same path DocumentViewer already uses.
  5. Where it lives. Site: /trips/:tripId/payments, linked from the Money card. App: pushed from the Dashboard's Money section. Both gated on payments.view_status.
Done when

An operator can answer "when did Maya pay, and how much" without opening Stripe, and hand their accountant a CSV.

G3

The trip summary tiles

App + Site No database work Small
Spec

Trip summary — Payments collected $16,800 of $28,000 · Fully paid 2/9, $7,000 total · Travelers 7/12. (Frame 39433.)

Today

No "fully paid" count anywhere, and no travelers-against-capacity: max_participants is never read in TripDashboardTab.tsx, and the Travelers heading shows a bare count.

Plan
  1. Derive, don't fetch. All three come from TripMoney and the trip row. Fully paid = travelers whose every step is paid, over travelers who have a price at all; the dollar figure beside it is their sum.
  2. Put the arithmetic in the domain files, with tests. dashboard/dashboardWork.ts in the app and domain/money.ts on the site, mirrored — that is how late.ts and money.ts are already kept honest.
  3. Tell the truth about the third tile. participant_count deliberately excludes travelers still in onboarding, so a trip that is selling well reads as empty. The tile shows going / max_participants with a second line — "+3 in onboarding" — whenever that number is above zero. This is the one place in the product where the gap is visible to the person it confuses, and it costs a line of copy to close.
  4. Placement. Top of the app's Dashboard tab and top of the site's trip page, above the travelers list. These are the numbers the page exists for, so they lead.
Done when

Both surfaces open on the same three figures, and a trip with eight people mid-onboarding no longer looks like a trip with two.

G4

Editing the trip's cancellation policy while it is still empty

App + Site Migration Blocked by D2 Medium
Spec

Only before any traveler joined: replace waiver (for this trip specifically), edit cancellation policy (for this trip specifically).

Today

The waiver half is built — ManageRequirementsSheet.tsx:404 calls replaceWaiverPdf, and guard_waiver_replacement narrows it to an empty trip. The policy half is not: cancellation_preset is written in exactly one place, CreateTripFlowA.tsx:2368, at publish. After that it is unreachable on both surfaces.

Plan

Copy the waiver's own machinery rather than inventing a second kind of freeze. The waiver guard is the right shape and the right precedent — policy for who, trigger for when.

  1. Migration — guard_trip_cancellation_policy(). A BEFORE UPDATE trigger on group_trips that fires only when a cancellation_* column actually changes, and then refuses unless all three hold: the caller is host_id (D2); there are no participants besides the host; and there are no rows in organized_trip_policy_consents for this trip. That third test is the load-bearing one, for the same reason it is in the waiver guard — someone may have already ticked terms that are about to change under them.
  2. Reuse the sheets. CancellationPolicySheet exists and is already shared between operator setup and settings. Point it at the trip's columns instead of operator_settings; the site has the same component on its settings page.
  3. Entry points. App: a row in OperatorTripEditScreen, next to the waiver. Site: the trip page, beside the requirements editor. Both hide the row rather than disable it once the trip is not empty — a disabled control invites the question, and the answer is a paragraph.
  4. Say what happened when it is refused. The waiver guard's message names the count ("2 travelers on this trip"). Match it.
Done when

An operator who published with the wrong refund terms can fix them until the first person joins, and cannot after — enforced in Postgres, not in a screen.

G5

Deadlines on crew paperwork

App + Site Migration Medium
Spec

Set required docs — deadlines will be similar to rest of travelers. Changing deadlines will effect the crew members deadlines accordingly.

Today

Crew paperwork has no deadline at all, deliberately. ensureStaffRequirements pins every staff row to skip_at_onboarding: 'must_have', which the table's CHECK then forces to carry a null deadline, and staff_my_requirements() "deliberately returns no deadline and no skip state". The reasoning holds and should survive: locking a guide out of the trip two days before departure is worse than the missing certificate.

Plan

Both halves of the spec are satisfied by deriving the crew deadline rather than copying it. A staff requirement is already a separate row of the same kind on the same trip — uq_organized_trip_req_kind_per_trip is unique per (trip, kind, audience) since 14 August — so the traveler row of that kind is right there to read. Nothing to sync, and no second clock to drift.

  1. Migration. Widen staff_my_requirements() to return deadline_days_before and a resolved due_date, left-joined from the sibling audience = 'traveler' row of the same kind on the same trip. A staff-only kind with no traveler sibling — a first-aid certificate, say — returns null, exactly as now.
  2. Flagged, never gated. The date is shown and can read as overdue. Nothing about it blocks a guide, and no reminder job is wired to it in this pass. Say that in the migration comment so the next person does not "fix" it.
  3. Surfaces. A due date and a Late pill in StaffPaperworkSection and the app's staff sheet; the same in the site's CrewPaperwork and on the crew list, so a Manager can see who is behind without opening each person.
  4. Free consequence. Editing a traveler deadline in either requirements editor moves the crew's with it, because there was never a second copy. That is the spec's second sentence, and it needs no code.
Done when

A photographer sees the same passport deadline the travelers see, moving the travelers' deadline moves theirs, and neither one is ever locked out of the trip over it.

G6

Managing yourself

App + Site Migration Blocked by D3 Medium
Spec

Manage self — edit your own description.

Today

Nobody can. ots_write covers all four verbs with staff.manage (20260901000000_co_operator_role.sql:250). And the operator has no staff row to edit in the first place — nothing creates one for the trip's owner, which is also why an operator trip shows no face on its overview at all unless they add themselves by hand.

Plan
  1. Migration, two parts. A second UPDATE policy scoped to user_id = auth.uid() and revoked_at is null, and — because RLS cannot exclude columns from a row — a BEFORE UPDATE trigger that refuses any change to role_key, user_id, revoked_at or listed when the caller does not hold staff.manage. Without the trigger, the policy hands every guide the ability to make themselves a co-operator. Same split as the waiver and dates guards.
  2. App. "Your details" in the trip menu for anyone with a staff row — title and bio, nothing else. It sits beside "Your paperwork", which is already gated the same way.
  3. Site. The same two fields on the crew page, on your own row.
  4. Add the operator to their own crew. One tap in the crew sheet that creates the owner's operator-tier row, pre-filled from their profile. This is what makes "manage self" mean anything for the operator, and it closes the separate hole where a trip with a paid-for crew section shows nobody running it.
Done when

A guide can fix their own bio, cannot touch their tier, and the operator appears on their own trip.

G7

Export every document on the trip

Site No database work Blocked by D4 Small
Spec

Manage trip, always: … export all docs.

Today

The site exports one requirement across everyone (RequirementPage.tsx:104) or one traveler's whole set (TravelerPage.tsx:116). There is no "everything". The app exports one file at a time (DocumentViewer.tsx:280).

Plan
  1. One button on the site's trip page. downloadAll already takes a list and a zip name, downloads sequentially on purpose, and reports progress. Feed it every traveler × every requirement.
  2. Folder per traveler inside the zip. A flat sixty-file archive is not an export, it is a pile. uniqueName already handles two travelers with the same name; folders make it unnecessary as well as safe.
  3. Confirm before starting. Name the count and say it will take a while. Sixty signed URLs fetched in series is a minute, and a button that appears to do nothing for a minute gets pressed again.
  4. Gate on data.export, as the two existing exports are.
  5. App. Per-traveler and per-requirement export only, and a line on the trip-wide entry point pointing at the desktop — see D4.
Done when

An operator hands a hotel one zip, sorted by guest, in one click.

G8

Opening a traveler's real profile

App + Site No database work Small
Spec

View traveler's personal profiles. Change: clicking members opens the personal full profile, not the surf-travel one.

Today

The app has no profile view from the dashboard at all — tapping a traveler opens the document review flow and stops there. The site shows age · country · surf level · board (TravelerPage.tsx:154), which is precisely the surf-travel card the spec says to replace.

Plan
  1. App. The wiring already exists — onViewUserProfile(userId, fromTripId) is threaded through every trip screen in RootNavigator. Add a tappable identity header at the traveler level of DocumentReviewScreen that calls it. This is the cheapest item in the plan.
  2. Site. Widen fetchProfiles to the fields the app's profile screen shows — photo, name, age, where they are from, their bio — and render them as a profile, not a stat line. Gate on travelers.view_profiles.
  3. Keep the surf-travel numbers. They are useful to a guide planning sessions; they stop being the whole profile. Group stats already live in "About this group" on both surfaces — the per-person card should read as a person.
Done when

From either dashboard, one tap on a traveler's name shows the person, not their board type.

Part two

Ten that exist on one surface

The spec's rule is one line — all functions and views should exist in both the mobile and desktop version — and it overturns a decision the site was built on. Its own spec still reads "desktop is read/review only", amended four times since; crew management and cancellation are recorded there as app-only, decided by Ohad on 13 August. That section needs rewriting as part of this work, not quietly contradicted by the code.

The good news is that almost none of it needs the database. The site's Rule 1 was rewritten on 19 August to say what it always meant: the boundary is RLS, not this project's restraint. Every item below writes through a table or an RPC the app already uses.

P1–P3

The three that make the site usable on its own

Site No database work Small, together
Why first

Today an operator sitting at a desktop cannot let anybody onto their trip, cannot tell anybody anything, and cannot call it off. Those are not advanced features — they are the three verbs a season needs, and each is a day's work at most because the app has already built the hard part.

Plan
  1. Join requests. A card on the trip page, and a count in the trips list so it is visible before you open anything. Approve and decline are one UPDATE each with status, reviewed_at, reviewed_by. Do not port the app's chat side-effect: on an operator trip approval grants onboarding, not a seat, and the app already skips it for exactly that reason.
  2. Cancel. A destructive-styled action behind a typed confirmation, then the same result screen the app shows — every refund listed, blocked ones named with the reason, and a retry. trip-cancel is safe to call twice by design, which is what makes the retry honest. Gate on trip.cancel.
  3. Admin updates. Read for everyone who can see the trip, post behind updates.send. This is also the answer to chat for now — see D5.
Done when

An operator can run a full season from the desktop without reaching for their phone.

P4–P6

The three the site chose not to build

Site No database work Small to medium
Today

Each is a written decision, not an oversight. AddCrewDialog.tsx:15: "a Listed credit wants a photo upload this project has no path for." CrewMemberDialog.tsx:135: "Their photo can be changed in the app." The site's spec records the same for the waiver, under a rule that files never go into <trip_id>/ from the browser.

Plan
  1. Photos (P4). There is a path, and it is not a storage policy problem: crew photos go through the image-upload edge function, which hands back a signed S3 URL. Both steps are fetch. Port uploadImageToS3's browser branch, drop the compression step for a canvas resize, and the Listed-crew form works — name, photo, role, description.
  2. Invite links (P5). create_staff_invite returns the link. The app shares it over WhatsApp; the site copies it to the clipboard, which is what a desktop does with a link.
  3. Waiver (P6). The storage policy on <trip_id>/operator/ gates on is_trip_host, so the browser is allowed. What blocks it is a line in the site's own spec, and that line needs an amendment — the fifth, in the format the previous four already use. Note in it that this is the one prefix the browser may write to, and why: the waiver is an operator document, not a traveler's.
Done when

Crew is managed end to end from the site, and the site's spec says so instead of saying the opposite.

Part three

What order, and what runs beside what

Four migrations, and they touch four different tables — they can be written and applied in any order. The client work splits cleanly by surface, so the app and the site can be built in parallel throughout.

Now, needing nobody

No decision · no migration

G8 (profile from the dashboard) and G3 (summary tiles) on the app; P1, P2, P3 on the site. Five items, none of which touch the same file as another, and together they are the difference between a desktop that reviews documents and one that runs a trip.

Once D1, D2 and D3 are answered

Three migrations, one per decision

G1 (emergency contact), G4 (trip cancellation policy), G6 (manage self). Each is a migration plus two small screens, and each is independent of the other two. G5 (crew deadlines) has no decision blocking it and can start alongside them — it is the fourth migration and the only one that changes an existing function.

Then

No database work

G2 (payments page) on both surfaces — the largest of the eight, and the one that wants a real design pass rather than a port. G7 and P10 together, since both are export and D4 answers both at once. P4, P5, P6 on the site.

Last, or not at all

Behind their own evidence

P7 and P8 complete parity. P9 — chat on the desktop — should be looked at again only after P3 has been in an operator's hands for a while. It may turn out that admin updates were the whole ask.

Boundaries

What this plan does not touch

Three things in the spec drawings are already built and were checked, and are listed here so nobody re-opens them: no "about the operator" section on operator trips; no destination-and-stay familiarity in the operator create flow; the commit feature off on operator trips. All three carry the spec reference in the code comment that made them.

Two more deviations were found and are deliberately left alone. A Manager cannot edit the trip price, though the spec's "anything besides dates & destinations" would allow it — the database authorises money on host_id and would refuse them anyway. And the group's surf and travel stats reach crew through a Dashboard tab rather than through Plan; the content is the spec's, the shelf is not. Both are worth a sentence in a review, neither is worth a migration.

Onboarding step order is the one small thing left unfixed: the code walks passport before the waiver, and visa before flights. The spec's order is the better one for a traveler — pay, sign, then the uploads — and it is a one-line change to REQUIREMENT_ORDER. It is not listed above because it is smaller than the sentence describing it.

Eighteen items. Four migrations, five decisions, and one honest exception to the both-surfaces rule. The largest single build in it is a page that reads data both apps already hold.
