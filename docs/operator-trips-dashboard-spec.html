Operator Trips — Dashboard Spec

The operator's screen for one trip: who paid, who uploaded, who needs a look. Draft, 2 August 2026.

What this is. The operator opens a trip and sees how it is going in one screen. Numbers on top, a full page behind every number, and a page per traveler. Read and act — it does not replace trip creation or trip editing.
Most of the data is already in the database. Requirements, documents, waivers and medical forms went live on 24 July. Approve and reject already work in the app. The dashboard screen itself does not exist yet.
Six questions closed on 2 August (Ohad). Medical gets export · purged files leave the row behind · exports are not logged · the approval step is in · desktop is a small separate site. Only two things are still open — see §8.
Money is blocked. The payment ledger was never applied. Until payments are decided, the money tile shows nothing. Everything else can ship without it.

1 · Where it lives

Entryoperator · mobile firstagreed

Like App Store Connect: a list of your trips, then a space per trip.

  • Mobile — everything. This is the product.
  • Desktop — yes, and it stays small. A separate simple website on Netlify, built later. It may live in its own codebase. Read, review and export only. No editing, no messaging.

An operator trip is a normal group_trips row with hosting_style = 'C'. There is no separate operator trips table.

Because desktop is its own site, it does not revive the React Native web build. It talks to the same Supabase project and the same RPCs. Nothing in the mobile app has to change for it to exist.

Needs reviewtop of the screendecided

One line: "12 documents waiting for you". Tap it and you get the list, oldest first.

  • It is a shortcut, not a queue you must clear.
  • Nothing happens on its own. No auto-approve, no timers, no chasing the operator.
  • Approve is one tap inside the document viewer. Bulk approve is in v1.
  • We do not push a notification for every upload. 60 pushes a trip and the operator mutes us. This count is the signal.

The traveler sees "waiting for the operator", so the wait is honest.

Confirmed 2 Aug: this whole approval step is not in SPEC.md — Eyal's document has no approved state and no review step. It is added scope, and it is in. "Done" means approved, not merely submitted.

Moneyblockedwaiting

Planned numbers: paid in full, total collected (₪ and $), still owed, paid deposit only, paid in installments.

  • Numbers are counted from an append-only ledger, never written straight from a webhook. That is what makes them safe when a webhook arrives twice or late.
  • The ledger table, deposit_amount and price_snapshot are not in the database yet.
  • For Israeli operators the money runs through their own gateway, so these totals are only as good as what their gateway reports back.

Until payments land: hide the tile. Do not show a fake zero.

Documentsthe main tiledata ready

One line per requirement: received and approved, both shown.

  • "Received 15/15 · approved 3/15" makes the gap clearly the operator's own backlog, not a traveler problem.
  • Expected = trip members with role member. Rejected uploads do not count as received.
  • Waiver and medical are not uploads. Show them as a short line: signed 13/15, form filled 11/15.
  • Custom requirements go in a separate Other requirements list, one line each. No big tile, no export column.

Medical flagscounts onlydata ready

Small counts, no names, no medical text on this screen: "3 injuries", "2 allergies", "5 diet notes".

  • Each count opens a view-all page.
  • The view-all page has export (decided 2 Aug). Same rule as every other tile — the operator has to hand a diet and allergy list to a hotel or a cook.
  • Never send medical text to an Edge Function, analytics or Swelly. Export is a direct download only.

This overrides SPEC.md §7, which said medical is view-only with no export.

3 · Detail page per tile

Every tile gets its own page: the full list, every traveler, and export. No exceptions — medical included.

  • Export means the real files, not a summary sheet. Operators have to send passports on to hotels and visa agents.
  • Accepted cost: our 30-day delete only bounds Swellyo. A downloaded copy is the operator's and lives on.
  • Files are private. Short-lived signed URLs only.
  • Exports are not logged (decided 2 Aug). No download history, no "who took what" record.
After the 30-day purgedecided 2 Augdecided

The row stays, the file does not. The purge deletes the file from storage and leaves the document row in place.

  • Counts do not change. "15/15 received · 15/15 approved" still reads 15/15 after the purge. History does not rewrite itself.
  • The row shows "File deleted after 30 days" instead of a thumbnail. View and export are gone for that row.
  • Passport typed fields (name, nationality, expiry) survive the purge, so expiry warnings and repeat travelers still work.
  • Nothing is re-requested. A purged file is done, not missing.
Already modelled: file_deleted_at on the document row, surfaced as fileDeleted in ReviewItem.

Knock-on from the medical export decision. Once the view-all page exports everyone's medical, keeping the single-traveler card view-only protects nothing — the same data leaves through the other door. So medical is view + export in both places. If you meant export on the aggregate page only, say so and this row goes back to view-only.
Before medical export shipsworth 10 minutes

Exporting medical data is a bigger step than exporting a passport scan, and the specs were written the other way round. Two cheap things to settle first:

  • Tell travelers. waiver-medical.md promises the medical form is collected to run one trip and is view-only for the operator. If the operator can now download it, the consent text the traveler reads must say so before they type anything.
  • Keep the purpose limit. Export is a direct file download and nothing else. Medical text still never reaches analytics, an Edge Function, or Swelly.

Neither blocks the build. Both are much cheaper to do now than after the first trip has real data in it.

Delete + reclaimone actiondecided

Reject and delete + reclaim are the same button. It deletes the file, marks the row rejected, and re-opens the task for the traveler with a notification.

  • A reason is optional. Next to it there is a Send a message button, so the operator can explain in one tap.
  • The row stays with rejected_at and the note. Re-upload replaces the row. There is no reject history — only the latest note.
  • There is no "not approved but keep the file" state.
Other actions
  • Message — mobile only.
  • Remove from trip — the action is agreed, what happens to money already paid is not.
  • Send a reminder — nice to have. Mostly covered by auto-chasing.

6 · Build order

  1. Snapshot screen — needs-review line, documents tile, medical tile, surf tile. All data exists.
  2. Detail page per tile — full list + view. No export yet.
  3. Traveler page — profile, file, medical, message, remove.
  4. Export — real files, signed URLs, medical included.
  5. Money tile — only after the payment ledger exists.
  6. Desktop site — a separate small Netlify site. Review and export, the two things that hurt on a phone. Own codebase, same Supabase.

7 · Not in v1

  • No auto-approve, no deadlines that escalate on their own.
  • No export logging or download history.
  • No staff roles — only the operator sees the dashboard.
  • No cross-trip view. One trip at a time.
  • No charts. Counts and lists.
  • No desktop in the first release. It comes after mobile works.

8 · Still open

Two left. Neither blocks starting the snapshot screen.

  1. Custom requirements on the dashboard. Operators can define their own items. The tiles are built around passport, visa, insurance, flights. Interim: an "Other requirements" list, one line each, with its own received and approved counts. How they are properly counted and exported — needs Eyal & Ohad.
  2. Removing a traveler who already paid. The action is agreed, the money is not. Ties into refunds and chargeback liability. TBD — moves with the payments decision, same as the money tile.
