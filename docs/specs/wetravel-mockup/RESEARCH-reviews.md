# WeTravel — what users say (research lane 4)

## Ratings, and why the spread is the finding

| Platform | Score | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Capterra / GetApp / Software Advice (one dataset) | 4.8 | 527 |
| Trustpilot | 3.9 | 512 — but **10% are 1-star** |
| Reviews.io | 2.8 | 63 — profile carries a *fake positive reviews removed* warning |
| PissedConsumer | 1.2 | 7 |

Capterra is vendor-solicited (reviewers name their account manager by first
name). Trustpilot also catches travelers and rejected applicants. The 4.8 is a
curated funnel.

## Praised, in order

1. **Human support / a named account manager.** The #1 theme by a distance.
2. **Payment plans and installments with automatic reminders.** The most-named
   single feature. Nobody attacks this — it is the moat.
3. **Getting paid internationally.** Dominant among LatAm, Africa, SE Asia
   operators: "Now I can charge to all my worldwide customers."
4. **Admin time saved** — "It's like I have a fully-staffed back office."
5. **Fast setup** — a trip live in 15 minutes.
6. **Looks professional** — makes a one-person operator look like a company.
7. Passing processing fees to the traveler.
8. Visible ship velocity.

## Complaints, in order

1. **Withheld funds / payout holds.** Existential — operators cannot pay hotels
   with money already collected. Appears *inside 5-star reviews* as the sole
   con, which makes it structural, not a support outlier. €50k frozen 8 months
   in one case.
2. **No phone support.** Compounds #1 — you cannot escalate a frozen $50k by
   email. Note the contradiction: support is both the #1 praise (onboarding)
   and #2 complaint (payments/risk).
3. **Stacked fees.** Not the headline rate — the layering. "$800 + 3% booking +
   3% card." Travelers punish the *operator* for the surcharge.
4. **FX markup on payouts.** The sharpest quote in the corpus: a €41 transfer
   fee where the forced exchange rate added **€250** over the bank's rate.
5. **KYC denial with no reason and no appeal.** Identical boilerplate to many
   operators. Users build entire trips, then get denied and lose the work.
6. **No organizer mobile app.** Every competitor attacks this.
7. **Rigidity** — trip pages, PDFs, forms, staff permissions.
8. **Reporting and invoicing.** "Invoices, invoices, invoices. I need to be able
   to create an invoice." Calendar shows open inventory, not real bookings.
9. **Chargebacks** — operator eats 100%; WeTravel does not fight them.
10. **English-only UI** despite a heavily LatAm/European base.
11. Too many automated emails; group emails not delivered.
12. **Traveler UX friction** — duplicate bookings because travelers can't find
    how to make a second payment; no save-and-resume; no split across two cards.
13. Slow payout rails — 6-day SEPA, 10-payee cap, no non-SWIFT banks, the debit
    card was killed and operators are still angry.

## Most-requested features

Faster/predictable payouts with a published hold policy · organizer mobile app ·
real invoicing · customizable reporting · localized UI · group-level accounting ·
save-and-resume checkout + a Sign In button on every trip page · split payment
across cards · granular staff permissions · move a participant between trips ·
better trip-page/PDF design control · true website embedding + custom domain ·
CRM/accounting integrations · trip folders/archiving · conditional form logic ·
affiliate program · chargeback defense · multiple hosts per retreat.

## Who they are

Micro and small businesses — solo operators to 10 staff, essentially no
enterprise. Verticals: multi-day group tours and DMCs, **wellness/yoga
retreats**, sports and adventure (cycling, moto, surf, dive), student travel,
faith-based travel. Geography is the distinctive part: **US organizer +
international operator**, with a huge LatAm presence. For the non-US half the
whole value prop is "I can finally accept US credit cards" — which is exactly
why the FX markup and payout holds cut so deep.

## Competitors' consistent line

*WeTravel is a payments product wearing a booking product's clothes.* No OTA
distribution, no channel manager, no POS, no website builder, thin ops and
costing, no organizer mobile app. YouLi attacks the UX and the fund holds;
Travefy is taking the itinerary layer even inside WeTravel accounts.

## What this means for the mockup

The mockup should show the parts that earn the love — payment plans, the
installment schedule, automated reminders, the professional trip page — at full
fidelity, because that is the product's real center of gravity. The pain points
are mostly policy and operations rather than screens, so they do not need to be
drawn, but they tell us which screens matter: payouts, fee disclosure, and the
document/paperwork loop.
