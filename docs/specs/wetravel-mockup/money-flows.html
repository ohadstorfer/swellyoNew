Where the money stops on its way to a WeTravel operator

A traveller's payment passes through five holders before it reaches the operator's own bank account. Something is taken at four of them. This page lists every stop, every figure, and how firmly each figure is known.

01

The shape of it

One sentence, then the detail.

The traveller's money never lands in the operator's bank account. It lands in a wallet the operator holds inside WeTravel, and the operator has to pull it out.

That single fact explains most of the rest of this page. It is why WeTravel can offer free instant supplier payments, why it can freeze an account, why the exchange rate is theirs to set, and why the operator's bank is the last stop rather than the first.

Two things follow that are easy to miss:

WeTravel holds no payments licence of its own.

Searching the full Terms and Privacy Policy returns zero occurrences of "money transmitter", "merchant of record", "escrow" or "custody". Instead the Terms appoint WeTravel as the organizer's "limited payment collection agent" and state that on receipt "the Traveler's payment obligation is extinguished." That is the standard agent-of-payee exemption. It operates under its vendors' licences — Stripe, Airwallex, Corpay, Mangopay — not its own. Published

There are four processors, not one.

The Terms clause everyone quotes says payment services are "among others provided by Stripe". Those two words are dropped by every second-hand summary. Airwallex and Corpay each get their own binding clause; Mangopay appears on the sub-processor list and nowhere else. Published

02

The five stops

Follow one payment from a traveller's card to an operator's bank. Each stop is a different company holding the money.

1

The traveller's bank or card

Their own bank · Visa / Mastercard / Amex / a local rail

The traveller picks a currency at checkout — WeTravel prices trips in about 36 currencies and accepts payment in 19 to 21. Their default is guessed from their location.

Taken here

  • Foreign-transaction fee, if the charge currency isn't theirs2–3% Not knowable

This one goes to their bank, not to WeTravel, and appears on no WeTravel screen. It is charged for real and the range is a common industry band, not a number anyone publishes per-traveller.

2

The processor

Stripe · Airwallex · Corpay (Cambridge Mercantile) for cross-border

Which one depends on the currency, and the seam is visible on the traveller's own bank statement: USD, EUR and GBP show a WT* prefix and settle on Stripe; AUD, CAD, HKD, SGD, MXN, PLN and a dozen more show no prefix and settle on Airwallex. Outbound international wires show as Cambridge Mercantile Corp.

Taken here — the processing fee, passed through at WeTravel's published rates

  • Local bank rail (ACH, SEPA, iDEAL, BACS, BECS, PAD, SPEI)0%
  • Visa / Mastercard, card issued locally1.5% – 3.4%
  • Visa / Mastercard, card issued abroad3.25% – 3.9%
  • American Express2.9% – 3.9%
  • Incoming wire transfer$25 / €25

Full breakdown in §03. Published

3

WeTravel's holding accounts

Wells Fargo · JPMorgan Chase, in WeTravel's name

The money is now pooled with every other operator's money in bank accounts WeTravel controls. The operator's balance is a line in WeTravel's ledger, not a segregated account.

Taken here

  • WeTravel booking fee, per transactionrate + $0.30, min $1.50
  • Currency conversion, if any leg changes currencystated $0.00 Not knowable

The Terms describe the position exactly: "The Trip Organizer controls the funds… however… the Trip Organizer is not entitled to any of the Trip Cost without WeTravel's consent." Nominal control, actual custody. Published

4

The operator's WeTravel wallet

A balance inside WeTravel · one per currency, 23 currencies possible

Money sits here until the operator moves it. There is no automatic payout schedule — every payout is initiated by hand. From this wallet the operator can pay themselves, pay a supplier instantly and free, spend on a virtual Visa card, or convert between their own currencies.

Taken here

  • Holding the balancenothing
  • Transfer to another verified WeTravel account (a supplier)free, seconds
  • Convert between your own currency walletsstated $0.00 Not knowable

This wallet is the actual product. Free instant supplier payment is only possible because WeTravel is holding both sides' money already — no bank rail is crossed. An operator who has moved their whole supply chain in here cannot leave without rebuilding it.

5

The operator's own bank

Local bank account · US debit card · international wire

The last stop, and the first point at which the money is unambiguously the operator's. Getting here requires full verification (§08) — most new accounts cannot do it at all for the first month.

Taken here

  • Local bank payout, USD — 1 business dayfree
  • Local bank payout, EUR / CAD — 2–3 business daysfree
  • Instant payout to linked US debit card — cap $3,000/day1.5%, min $0.50
  • International wire — 3–5 business days$15
  • Intermediary and beneficiary bank fees on that wireNot knowable

22 currencies have a free local rail. Everything else falls back to the $15 wire, and 48 currencies — including BRL, ARS and COP — cannot be wired at all. Published

The two numbers that matter most

Local bank rails cost nothing. ACH, SEPA, iDEAL, BACS, BECS, PAD and SPEI are all 0%. On a $2,000 booking that is the difference between $0 and $58.

A card issued in the wrong country costs roughly double. A European card paying a EUR trip is 1.5%. The same card paying a USD trip is 3.9% — and the traveller's own bank adds its foreign-transaction fee on top.

PayPal, Klarna, Affirm, Afterpay — there is no buy-now-pay-later anywhere in the product. Also absent: Pix, Boleto, OXXO, Multibanco, MobilePay, Alipay, WeChat Pay, Cash App Pay. Published

04

The WeTravel fee

The one number WeTravel does not print. Here is what is known, and exactly how firmly.

Their pricing page lists every card rate in a neat table and then writes "+ WeTravel Booking Fee" with a Learn more link and no figure. The link goes to a sales-call form. Two facts are stated publicly and no more:

Everything WeTravel publishes about its own fee

  • Charged per transaction, not per bookingPublished
  • Minimum$1.50 USD Published

What the shape is, from three real observations

Three live checkouts — one on a third-party operator's public page, two on a demo account — resolve the formula, if not the rate:

Charge observedFee shown1% + $0.302% + $0.30min $1.50
$1,000 deposit, third-party live trip$20.30$10.30$20.30 ✓—
$150, demo account$1.80$1.80 ✓$3.30—
$100, demo account$1.50$1.30 → floored$2.30$1.50 ✓

Three for three. The fee is max($1.50, rate × amount + $0.30), charged on every transaction. Measured

The rate itself is account-dependent, and the mapping is a guess.

1% on the demo account, 2% on the third-party account. Public secondary sources describe it as "as low as 1% + $0.30", which points at 1% for Pro, 2% for Basic — but nobody states that, and it could just as easily be negotiated per account or scaled by volume. Inferred

Treat any total that includes this rate as a range: the same trip costs twice as much on one account as the other.

Payment plans split the fee, they don't avoid it

On the $4,900 trip observed above, the $1,000 deposit carried $20.30 and the $3,900 balance carries $78.30 on the same formula — $98.60 total. A four-installment plan pays the $0.30 four times instead of once. Whether the $1.50 minimum bites depends on the rate: at 2% it never does above $60, but on a 1% account a plan of ten $80 payments is floored to $1.50 each — $15.00 — where the same $800 paid in one go costs $8.30. Splitting a small trip into many installments can nearly double the fee.

Who pays it is a per-trip switch

Trip Builder ▸ Settings has two independent radio pairs: payment fees paid by organizer / participant, and WeTravel fee paid by organizer / participant. Either or both can be passed to the traveller. To the traveller the WeTravel fee is labelled Service Fee; the processing fee only appears once they pick a card or wire — local bank rails add nothing, so nothing is shown. Published

No WeTravel fee is charged on incoming wires, on cash or external payments, or on the act of setting up a payment plan.

The constraints that bite

  • Instant payout is unavailable on a first-time payout account, needs a real US debit card linked to an active local bank, and rejects prepaid cards. Capped at $3,000 a day.
  • A local USD payout needs an SSN or EIN. Without either, wire is the only route out. Stripe is legally required to identity-check the operator once cumulative payouts pass "a few thousand dollars", which can stall a first large payout by days.
  • Wires are irreversible — "once you submit the wire transfer, you will not be able to cancel it" — must go to an account in the operator's own business name, and are capped at $500,000 a day.
  • Supplier transfers need both sides independently verified. Free and instant, but only inside the network.
  • Conversion has floors and a ceiling: roughly 20 units minimum for majors, 2,000 for JPY and HUF, and 300,000 USD-equivalent per day.

Connecting your own Stripe account turns four products off

WeTravel offers a mode where funds go straight to the operator's own Stripe account and "WeTravel has no visibility or access." Choosing it disables international wire, the WeTravel Card, supplier transfers and multi-currency checkout — which proves those four run on WeTravel's own aggregated merchant accounts rather than passing through. Published

07

The costs that appear on no table

Two of them, and between them they can exceed every published fee combined.

The exchange-rate spread

Currency conversion is advertised as free, and the Transaction Report literally logs the fee as 0. The margin is not in a fee line — it is in the rate. The Terms describe the mechanism plainly:

WeTravel updates the base exchange rate on a regular basis, but not on a real-time basis. In particular, WeTravel does not always change the base exchange rate immediately when its costs of foreign exchange change. Accordingly, the base exchange rate may not be identical to the applicable market rate in effect at the specific time a foreign currency conversion is processed.

WeTravel Terms of Service, 1 Dec 2025

WeTravel sets its own rate, refreshes it when it chooses, and keeps the gap. There is no percentage anywhere in the Terms — a full-text search of all 83,000 characters returns zero % figures. Published mechanism Size not knowable

Conversion is triggered in four situations, per the Terms: the display currency differs from the listing currency; the booking currency differs from the listing currency; the payout currency differs from the listing currency; or a booking that already involved a conversion is modified or cancelled.

One measured data point, and it is not small

An operator on Trustpilot (Apr 2025) measured €250 of hidden cost on a transfer whose stated fee was €41 — the undisclosed part being six times the disclosed part. This is one review, self-reported, with no invoice attached. It is the only quantified figure that exists publicly, and it should be treated as an anecdote that establishes an order of magnitude, not as a rate. Single unverified report

Corpay's own charges

The Terms hand the cross-border leg to Corpay and then say its costs are billed separately:

Cross-border transactions processed by Corpay may involve compliance with international payment regulations, foreign exchange adjustments, and banking network fees that vary by jurisdiction. Such charges are assessed directly by Corpay (Fleetcor) and its partners.

WeTravel Terms of Service, 1 Dec 2025

This is how banking-network costs reach the operator without ever appearing on WeTravel's published fee table. The published percentages do not describe the full cost of a cross-border payment. Published mechanism Size not knowable

08

When the money stops moving

Three separate mechanisms hold funds, and only one of them requires a reason.

Verification — the gate most new operators hit first

LevelDocumentsUnlocks
1 — SupplierGovernment ID + selfieReceive transfers only
2 — Trusted Partner+ proof of address, business registration, website or socialsListed in the Partner Hub, eligible for the WeTravel Card
3 — Trip Organizer+ travel registration detailsCollect money from travellers

Submission takes 5–15 minutes, review up to 3 business days, and each level is a separate process. WeTravel describes "partial approval" as the most common outcome for new users — and partial approval blocks international wire, the Card and supplier transfers outright. The exit is to wait until 30 days after the first trip has completed and then email support. It is not automated.

For a brand-new operator that is functionally escrow past the departure date. WeTravel never calls it that, and it is not written as a policy — it is the practical effect of the verification flow. Inferred from their own help text

Risk holds — up to 30 days, sometimes 180

Risk-based holds generally remain in place for up to 30 days from the date the payment was received… The hold may last longer than 30 days if the payment is deemed fraudulent and/or results in a disputed transaction or chargeback. In this case, we'll hold the payment until the matter is resolved (but no longer than 180 days).

WeTravel Terms of Service

Held funds stay in the balance and can still be used to issue refunds — they just cannot be paid out. The stated inputs are fraud warnings, account tenure, transaction activity, business type, past disputes and customer satisfaction. Published

The suspension clause — no cause, no notice, no explanation

We may, in our discretion and without liability to you, with or without cause, with or without prior notice and at any time, decide to limit, suspend, deactivate or delete your WeTravel Account… (c) we may temporarily place a hold on your account equivalent to total payments for upcoming and future trips, expected amount of refunds, or potential chargeback amounts… (e) we may refund your Travelers in full for any and all confirmed reservations, irrespective of preexisting cancellation policies… (g) you will not be entitled to any compensation for reservations or bookings (even if confirmed) that were cancelled.

WeTravel Terms of Service, "Suspension, Termination and Account Deletion"

This is not a standing rolling reserve. It is a discretionary hold triggered by suspension, and its ceiling is every payment collected for every upcoming trip. It is the contractual mechanism behind the "they froze our money with no explanation" reviews, and it reads exactly as it operates. Published, verbatim

09

Money going backwards

Refunds and chargebacks are where the operator, not WeTravel, absorbs the loss.

Refunds

  • The traveller always receives the full amount they paid.
  • The card processing fee is not recovered — it comes out of the operator's balance and is gone. On a $2,000 card booking that is $58 lost on a refunded trip.
  • Refunds land in 5–10 business days and cannot be reversed.
  • Bank rails hard-stop at 180 days after payment; 90 days for PIX, PayNow and BECS. After that a refund cannot be issued through the original rail at all.

An unresolved contradiction about the booking fee

The help centre says WeTravel refunds its own fee on a refund. The Terms of Service say "The Service Fee is always non-refundable." Both statements are WeTravel's own, and they cannot both be true as written.

The most likely reading is that the help centre describes current practice and the Terms reserve the right to change it — but that is a guess. On a $2,000 refunded booking the difference is about $40. Unresolved

Chargebacks — the operator carries all of it

When a Traveler files a dispute or chargeback with their bank, the Trip Organizer agrees that the full amount of the Trip Cost… will be automatically deducted from the WeTravel account… It typically takes up to 60 to 80 business days for the bank to come to a resolution… During this time, the funds are also temporarily returned to the Traveler.

WeTravel Terms of Service

60–80 business days is three to four months. The Terms also state the organizer is "fully liable to WeTravel for the full amount of all disputes or chargebacks… including any relevant costs and fines", and:

We reserve the right to debit your connected bank account in the event that your WeTravel account balance becomes negative. This happens when disputes are more than the balance in your account.

WeTravel Terms of Service

So the exposure is not capped by the wallet. Published

Why the operator carries it — inferred, not stated

WeTravel's verification article refers to connecting "a Stripe Standard account" and the Terms bind the operator to the Stripe Connected Account Agreement. In Stripe's model a Standard connected account is itself the merchant of record and carries dispute liability. That reading matches the dispute policy exactly — but WeTravel never uses the phrase "merchant of record" anywhere, so the mechanism is inferred from two adjacent facts. Inferred

There are no cancellation policy tiers

WeTravel has no structured cancellation policy feature. The policy is freeform text in the trip description, and the platform default when nothing is written is: "All payments paid through WeTravel are non-refundable." There is also no late fee anywhere in the product, and no automatic retry on a failed installment — a missed payment is simply added to the next one. Published

The honest summary

Every published percentage on this page is solid. The three costs that decide whether a cross-border booking is cheap or expensive — the FX spread, Corpay's charges and the correspondent bank fees — are all undisclosed. That is not an oversight in the research; it is the design of the pricing.
