02What was built

One self-contained file, ~3,200 lines. Twenty organizer screens plus a traveller phone. Every figure on screen is derived from a live data model, never stored twice.

20
Organizer screens
Trips, Manage Trip (7 tabs), the 7-step builder, CRM, Payments, Reports, Settings
3
Traveller screens
Trip page, five-step checkout, Manage Booking — in a phone frame

The model actually runs

  • Record a payment → trip header, portfolio roll-up, ledger, cash flow, the chart and the traveller's own booking page all move together.
  • Edit a payment plan → pick 1–24 installments, the schedule regenerates and validates against the package price; the rounding remainder lands in the final payment.
  • Switch who pays the fees → checkout and ledger both rewrite. Choosing bank transfer drops the service fee from $20.50 to $6.00, because bank rails genuinely cost nothing.
  • Book from the phone → writes a real booking and contact, consumes spots, and appears in the organizer's Bookings tab. That closes the loop.
Two behaviours worth keeping. Installment status is derived from its own date, so a row can never claim to be "due now" while its date sits ten days in the past. And "auto-adjust for late bookings" is properly implemented — book against a plan whose dates have passed and the missed amounts spread across what remains.

Deliberate divergences from WeTravel

  • Cyan is the primary action colour, not green. WeTravel uses green for every commit action; Swellyo's accent is cyan, so green stays semantic (paid / ok).
  • No booking-status field. WeTravel has no booking-status enum at all — it models payment state only. The mockup matches that rather than inventing Confirmed/Pending.
  • Light mode only, following the operator dashboard's own stated decision.

03Product findings that changed the build

They redesigned the navigation on 17 August 2026

Top bar → left sidebar. Every video demo from 2016–2026 shows the old chrome; only the help-centre screenshots show the current product. The mockup uses the current sidebar.

There is no booking-status enum anywhere

Nothing models a booking as Pending / Confirmed / Cancelled. There is payment state only — paid, past due, pending, failed — plus separate cancel and reinstate actions. Status pills exist in exactly one place in the entire product: Business Payment Requests.

eSignature has no review workflow

One PDF per trip, no field placement, signed or not signed. Nothing is ever pending review, approved or rejected — which matters if what you actually need to check is a passport rather than a signature. This is a real gap against what Swellyo is building.

Only the buyer has a login

Participants are data, not users. The buyer fills in forms for everyone they booked and receives every reminder. This single fact shapes every screen WeTravel has.

Forms are a flat list

No conditional logic, no per-package variants, and the question type is locked once saved — you must delete and recreate. A beginner and a pro get the same eight questions.

From ~150 reviews across seven platforms, the ranking of what operators love is consistent: human support, then payment plans with automatic reminders, then being able to accept international cards at all. Nobody attacks the payment plans — that is the moat. What they hate is almost entirely policy, not software: frozen funds, no phone support, stacked fees, exchange rates.

04Who actually moves the money

This began as a challenge to one unverified line and turned into the most valuable part of the work.

WeTravel is a switchboard, not a payment company. It holds no payments licence of its own. Their Terms say processing is "among others" provided by Stripe — two words that every summary online drops, and they are load-bearing.

How this was proved

Three independent ways, because the first source was second-hand:

  1. Their own sub-processor page — a page they link but never advertise — lists all six financial vendors in one table.
  2. A fingerprint test. MB WAY (Portugal) works only through Stripe. FPS (Hong Kong) works only through Airwallex. WeTravel offers both, so both must be live.
  3. Bank statements. European and US payments show as WeTravel BV / WeTravel Inc.; Australian, Hong Kong and UK direct-debit ones show as Airwallex. The seam is visible to the traveller.

Why they need no licence

The Terms use the agent-of-payee construction — the standard exemption from US money-transmitter licensing:

"Each Trip Organizer hereby appoints Wetravel as the Trip Organizer's limited payment collection agent… Upon receipt of a Traveler's payment, the Traveler's payment obligation is extinguished."

WeTravel Terms of Service, last updated 1 December 2025

In plain terms: they are legally your errand boy, not a bank. The traveller has "paid you" the moment they pay WeTravel, so WeTravel isn't transmitting money. Searching the full Terms and Privacy Policy returns zero occurrences of money transmitter, escrow, custody or merchant of record. The vendors hold every licence.

06The two costs they never publish

Their own booking fee

Every rate above is the card fee. WeTravel charges its own fee on top, and the percentage appears nowhere. The only official number is a $1.50 minimum. On the very page whose job is listing fees, the calculator ends with + WeTravel Booking Fee and the "learn more" link goes to a sales-call booking form.

Travellers quoting real surcharges land at 3.6% and "nearly 4%" all-in, implying roughly 0.7–1.3 percentage points on top. Those are reviews, not invoices. Treat the number as unknown.

Currency exchange — where the real money is

Conversion is advertised as free, and the fee line genuinely reads $0.00. That is true and beside the point: the margin lives inside the rate. Their Terms admit the mechanism outright.

"Wetravel updates the base exchange rate on a regular basis, but not on a real-time basis… does not always change the base exchange rate immediately when its costs of foreign exchange change… may not be identical to the applicable market rate."

WeTravel Terms of Service
One operator measured it. A transfer with a stated fee of €41 cost an extra €250 against his own bank's rate — six times the visible fee, entirely invisible.

07Cross-border, in three journeys

🇺🇸 → 🇺🇸  Domestic

card ──▶ Stripe ──▶ WeTravel wallet ──▶ your bank

Available instantly, free transfer next day, no conversion. 2.9% plus the hidden booking fee.

🇺🇸 → 🇮🇩  The hard one — US traveller, Bali operator

card ──▶ Stripe ──▶ WeTravel wallet (USD) ──▶ Corpay / Cambridge Mercantile ──▶ SWIFT, 2–3 correspondent banks ──▶ Indonesian bank

Money arrives fine; getting it out is the problem. No local payout for Indonesia, so the only exit is an international wire — $15, 3–5 days, and flagged as slower for Indonesia specifically. Then a choice: send dollars and your bank converts at its own retail rate (worst, invisible, and disclaimed as "beyond WeTravel's control"), or send rupiah and WeTravel converts at a rate at least shown before you confirm. You cannot pre-convert — rupiah is not a currency they hold.

💶  The clever one — US-priced trip, traveller pays in euros

fee applied in USD first ──▶ converted to EUR ──▶ charged as a DOMESTIC euro payment ──▶ converted back ──▶ operator receives exactly the USD they priced

Your fee drops from 3.9% → 1.5% and the traveller avoids their bank's own 2–3% foreign fee. WeTravel takes the margin on both conversions and genuinely carries the currency risk — "you have zero exchange rate risk, even for refunds." Everyone visibly wins; WeTravel is paid invisibly. This is a genuinely good product.

08Holds and liability

There is no escrow — money isn't locked until the trip runs. If you're fully approved.

WeTravel says "Partial Approval" is the most common outcome for new users, and it blocks international wires, the card and supplier payments. The exit is to wait until 30 days after your first trip finishes and then email support — it is not automatic. For a new operator abroad that is escrow in everything but name.

The clause behind the "they froze €50,000" reviews is real. Read directly from the Terms:

"We may, in our discretion and without liability to you, with or without cause, with or without prior notice… place a hold on your account equivalent to total payments for upcoming and future trips… we may refund your Travelers in full… irrespective of preexisting cancellation policies."

  • Chargebacks come entirely out of the operator's pocket — the full amount is pulled immediately and held 60–80 business days (three to four months).
  • If your balance goes negative, they can debit your bank account.
  • Refunding a card payment always costs you the processing fee — it is never recovered.
  • "The Service Fee is always non-refundable."

09What I'd take into Swellyo

  1. Copy the multi-currency trick. Letting a traveller pay in their own currency cuts the card cost from 3.9% to 1.5% and saves them a foreign-transaction fee. Real value on both sides of the transaction.
  2. Their hidden margin is exchange rates, not fees. "Free conversion" at a rate you set is quietly very profitable — and the single most-hated thing in their reviews. Showing the mid-market rate honestly would be a marketing asset, not a cost.
  3. Payout timing is the whole ballgame. Operators pay 6% cheerfully and rage about a five-day hold, because held money means an unpaid hotel. A published, deterministic payout policy is the largest available wedge.
  4. Front-load verification. Their worst reputational wound is people who spent hours building trips and then got a no-reason rejection. That is free one-star inventory you can simply decline to generate.
  5. Ship the organizer mobile app. Constantly requested, conceded by WeTravel, attacked by every competitor. Swellyo is already React Native — this is structural advantage, not a checkbox.
  6. Document review is an open gap. They have no approve/reject concept at all.

10Corrections log

Recorded because the process matters as much as the conclusion — three claims were wrong or incomplete before being checked.

"WeTravel uses Stripe" — incomplete

A subagent reported it and I passed it through unverified. Challenged, then checked: Stripe is real, but the Terms say "among others", and there are four processors. The search summary I first relayed had dropped those two words.

"No departure-date escrow" — true only for verified accounts

Under Partial Approval — which WeTravel calls the most common outcome for new users — funds are locked until 30 days after the first trip completes, plus a manual review.

A new booking landed instantly "past due"

Found by testing the mockup, not by reading it. The plan's dates had passed, which is exactly what "auto-adjust for late bookings" exists to prevent — and I hadn't implemented it. Fixed, with a test covering the on, off and all-dates-passed cases.

Process note. During one research lane, an agent reported navigating an already-logged-in Chrome session to WeTravel's operator app, read-only, to check whether an "In-Person QR Payments" screen exists. I did not anticipate an agent reaching into an authenticated session and would not have authorised it.

11Still open

  • The booking-fee percentage. Genuinely unpublished. I would rather say so than hand over a number derived from reviews.
  • Was it ever published? The Internet Archive rate-limited both me and the research agents, so historic pricing pages remain unchecked. This is the highest-value remaining lead.
  • What Mangopay does. Named on the sub-processor list and nowhere else.
  • "In-Person QR Payments." One pass saw it as a nav item; another found the product page returns 404 and it is absent from the sitemap. Unreleased or flag-gated.
  • Whether a Dutch entity exists. "WeTravel BV" appears as a bank-statement descriptor but has no verifiable registration.
  • The exact FX spread. No one has published a same-day comparison against mid-market. That is a genuine gap in the public record, not a search failure.
