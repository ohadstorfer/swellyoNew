Staff requirements & wallet delivery — spec & plan

12 August 2026 · rev 1 · operator trips (type C) · the two gaps left in the feature list

What we are building, in one breath.
  • Part A. A staff member can be asked for paperwork — passport, visa, a signed waiver — per person, not per trip. A guide flying in needs a visa; the local driver does not.
  • Part B. An operator can put a document into a traveler's wallet — tickets, itineraries, confirmations. Things the operator issues, never things the traveler is supposed to provide.
The line that runs through both parts. A document is evidence of who did something. A passport in the wallet means the traveler chose to hand it over; a signed waiver means the person bound by it agreed. Neither statement survives if somebody else can produce the document on their behalf. Both parts below are shaped by that, and Part B is narrower than it first sounds because of it.

Where this comes from

An audit of the operator-trips feature list on 12 Aug found everything built except two items: requirements for staff, and the operator putting documents in a traveler's wallet. Everything else — traveler onboarding, Stripe onboarding, the create-trip flow, the dashboard, staff roles and invites — already exists and is in fe7a167.

Part A — Requirements for staff

Staff exist (organized_trip_staff, five tiers, invites, promote/demote). What they cannot do is be asked for anything. Requirements today address travelers only.

Why per person, not per trip

Traveler requirements are a property of the trip: everybody who joins is asked the same things. Staff are not like that. On one trip the photographer flies in from abroad and needs a visa, the head guide needs a first-aid certificate that expires, and the local driver needs neither. A trip-wide list would ask all three for all of it, and the operator would train everyone to ignore the list — which is the failure mode that makes a requirements system worthless.

Trip-wide: "This trip requires a visa" → the driver who lives twenty minutes away is chased for a visa forever.
Per person: the operator ticks visa on the photographer only.

The rule

  • A requirement row gains an audience: traveler or staff. Existing rows are all traveler.
  • Traveler requirements apply to everyone who joins, as today. Nothing changes.
  • Staff requirements apply to nobody by default. The operator assigns each one to specific people.
  • A staff member fulfils them through the same screens travelers use. One upload path, one approval path, one storage bucket.
  • Deposit and balance are never staff requirements. Staff do not pay to work.

Data model

Two changes. An audience column on the requirement, and a join table saying who a staff requirement is for. 

alter table public.organized_trip_requirements
  add column if not exists audience text not null default 'traveler'
    check (audience in ('traveler','staff'));

-- Staff do not pay to work. Enforced here rather than in the UI, because a
-- 'pay' requirement pointed at staff would reach the checkout gate and try to
-- charge a member of the crew.
alter table public.organized_trip_requirements
  add constraint organized_trip_req_staff_never_pays
  check (audience = 'traveler' or req_type <> 'pay');

create table if not exists public.organized_trip_staff_requirements (
  id             uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  staff_id       uuid not null references public.organized_trip_staff(id) on delete cascade,
  requirement_id uuid not null references public.organized_trip_requirements(id) on delete cascade,
  assigned_by    uuid not null references auth.users(id),
  assigned_at    timestamptz not null default now(),
  unique (staff_id, requirement_id)
);
Why a join table and not a column on the staff row. Requirements are a set per person and the set changes — somebody is promoted from driver to guide and now needs the certificate. A join table makes assigning and un-assigning one row each, keeps the history of who assigned it, and means the fulfilment tables (organized_trip_travelers_documents, group_trip_acknowledgements) need no schema change at all — they already key on requirement_id and a user.

What we deliberately do not build

  • A second requirements engine. Staff resolve, upload, and get approved through the paths that already exist. If staff needed their own engine, the two would drift and one of them would rot.
  • Staff requirements gating anything. A traveler with unmet requirements cannot join the trip. A guide with an unmet requirement is flagged, not blocked — the operator hired them and knows the situation; a system that locks the guide out of the trip chat two days before departure is worse than the missing document.
  • Requirements on the operator themselves. They are the one holding the list.

Part B — Putting documents in a traveler's wallet

The wallet exists and is traveler-side: approved items land there and stay. What is missing is the operator being able to put something in it.

The thing to get right first

"Operator uploads to the wallet" is two features, and only one of them is safe.
  • Delivering a document the operator issues — a flight itinerary, a hotel confirmation, a packing list, an insurance certificate for the trip. Safe, useful, and the actual ask.
  • Filling in a document the traveler owes — uploading their passport for them, or ticking their waiver. Not built, on purpose.

The second breaks the evidence. Every requirement record exists to answer "did this person provide this, and when". A waiver especially: an agreement is only worth the record if the person bound by it made it, and an operator-signed waiver is not a waiver, it is a liability with a signature on it. The medical form is the same. So delivery is one-directional: the operator can hand a traveler a document, and can never satisfy a requirement on their behalf.

Allowed: operator uploads "El Salvador 26 — final itinerary.pdf" → appears in every traveler's wallet.
Allowed: operator uploads "Maya's flight confirmation.pdf" → appears in Maya's wallet only.
Refused: operator uploads a passport scan to satisfy Maya's passport requirement.

The rule

  • A delivered document is its own kind of row. It is never a requirement fulfilment and carries no requirement_id.
  • It is addressed either to the whole trip or to one traveler.
  • It needs no approval — the operator is the source, so there is nobody to approve it.
  • The traveler can open it, download it, and never delete it. It is a record they were given something.
  • Withdrawing one is the operator's action and leaves the row, marked withdrawn, for the same reason refunds keep their row.

Data model

create table if not exists public.organized_trip_delivered_documents (
  id            uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  trip_id       uuid not null references public.group_trips(id) on delete cascade,
  -- NULL = everybody on the trip. A named user = that traveler only.
  recipient_id  uuid references auth.users(id) on delete cascade,
  uploaded_by   uuid not null references auth.users(id),
  title         text not null check (length(title) between 1 and 200),
  note          text check (note is null or length(note) <= 1000),
  storage_path  text not null,
  size_bytes    integer,
  delivered_at  timestamptz not null default now(),
  withdrawn_at  timestamptz
);
Why not extend organized_trip_operator_documents. That table holds the operator's own paperwork — their default waiver, their insurance certificate — which travelers never see. Adding a recipient to it would put "documents nobody may read" and "documents delivered to a named traveler" in one table behind one RLS policy, and the first mistake in that policy exposes the insurance certificate. Different audiences deserve different tables.

Step B1 — Migration + storage

RLS: the operator and docs.view staff read all rows on their trips and insert; a traveler reads rows on trips they are in where recipient_id is null or recipient_id = auth.uid(), and withdrawn_at is null. Travelers never insert into this table — that is the one-directional rule, held in the database rather than the UI.

Reuses the existing documents bucket with a delivered/ prefix.

Step B2 — Operator UI

Dashboard tab and traveler rownot started

Two entry points, because the two cases are genuinely different:

  • Trip-wide — a Documents section on the dashboard tab: upload, title, optional note, goes to everyone.
  • One traveler — a Send document action on the traveler row, next to the existing Set price and refund actions.

Both use the file picker already wired for the waiver.

Step B3 — Traveler UI

The wallet gains a second sourcenot started

Delivered documents appear in the wallet alongside approved requirement items, marked From your operator so the two are never confused. A traveler must be able to tell "this is mine, I gave it" from "this is theirs, they gave me it" at a glance — the wallet is the one place both live.

A push when one arrives, using the notification plumbing that already exists.

Deploy order

  1. A1 migration — additive, safe on its own, nothing reads the new column yet.
  2. A2 + A3 + A4 ship together. An assignment nobody can see is worse than no assignment.
  3. B1 migration — additive and independent; can go at any point.
  4. B2 before B3? No — B3 first. A delivered document with nowhere to appear is a document the traveler never gets, and the operator has no way to know. Ship the reader, then the writer.

Out of scope, on purpose

  • Expiry on staff certificates. A first-aid certificate that lapses is a real problem and a real feature — dates, reminders, a re-upload flow. It is not this one.
  • Staff requirements across trips. Assignment is per trip. The same guide on four trips is asked four times, which is annoying and correct: the paperwork a trip needs is the trip's business.
  • Traveler-to-operator delivery. Travelers send documents by fulfilling requirements. A second channel would be a place for a passport to arrive with no record that it was asked for.
  • Blocking on staff paperwork — see Part A. Flag, do not gate.

How we will know it worked

  • A staff member assigned a visa requirement sees it, uploads it, and the operator approves it — through the traveler screens, with no staff-specific upload code.
  • Assigning a requirement to one crew member leaves the other three untouched.
  • A pay requirement cannot be created with audience = 'staff'; the database refuses it.
  • An operator uploads an itinerary to the trip and every traveler sees it in the wallet, marked as theirs.
  • A document sent to one traveler is invisible to the others — checked by querying as another traveler, not by looking at the screen.
  • There is no code path, anywhere, by which an operator upload sets requirement_id.
