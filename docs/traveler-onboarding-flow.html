Traveler onboarding — operator trips (type C)

A guided flow that starts the first time an approved traveler opens the trip.

The one rule: approval buys nothing but a ticket to the onboarding. No seat, no spot in the count, no membership. Until the three required items are done, the traveler sees exactly what a stranger sees — the public trip page, plus a Start onboarding button. They can still DM the organiser, because anyone can. Finishing onboarding is what makes them a member. It runs once — it never re-opens.

Rules the flow must obey

When does onboarding open?

Two doors, same flow:

  • “Start onboarding” on the “You’re in!” overlay, right after approval.
  • Opening the trip, any time, while the required items are unfinished.

Both need: approved participant of a type-C trip, and onboarding_completed_at is null.

When is it finished?

When the traveler reaches the last step and taps Go to the trip. That stamps onboarding_completed_at. It is finished even if every optional item was skipped — skipping is a valid outcome.

Backing out halfway leaves the stamp null, so it opens again next time — but every completed step is already saved, so it resumes where they stopped.

What does an unfinished traveler get?

Exactly what someone who never asked to join gets. Approval alone changes nothing about the UI. There is no new lock screen to design — the public trip page already is the lock screen.

The four skippable items gate nothing. Skipping all four still turns them into a full member.

The trap: “no participant row” makes onboarding impossible must read

“Approved but not a participant” sounds like don’t insert the row yet. That breaks everything. 15 places in the database gate on is_trip_participant(). With no row, an approved traveler cannot:

  • read the trip’s requirement list — they would not even see what to do
  • sign the waiver, or fill the medical form
  • upload a passport, insurance, visa or flight file
  • read or write anything in the documents bucket (a storage policy)

Rewriting those 15 policies to also accept “has an approved join request” is a lot of surface area on live, security-critical rules.

The cheap way to get exactly what you asked for recommended

Keep inserting the participant row on approval, and give it a status: 'onboarding' → 'active'. Then membership means three separate things, and only the last one flips at approval:

QuestionAnswered byAt approval
May I touch this trip’s documents?is_trip_participant()yes — all 15 policies keep working, zero rewrites
Do I take a spot?capacity triggerno — counts status = 'active' only
Am I in the group?participant_count + the UI flagno — same filter

Three small changes instead of fifteen: one column, one line in enforce_group_trip_max_participants(), one line in sync_group_trip_participant_count(). Default the column to 'active' so peer trips (A/B) are untouched.

On the client, TripDetailScreen already draws the whole member/non-member difference off one boolean, isApprovedMember. Point it at status = 'active' and the Plan tab, chat button and member list disappear together. DMs need no exception — they live outside the trip.

The race: paying for a spot that is already gone decide this

If the seat is only claimed at the end of onboarding, and the deposit is inside onboarding, then this happens:

10 spots. The operator approves 14 people. Twelve of them pay the deposit. The last two finish onboarding and are told “trip is full” — after their money has already left their account. That is two refunds and two very angry customers.

Fix — split the two moments: claim the seat the instant the deposit succeeds, not at the end of the flow. Money is the real commitment; the passport and the visa are paperwork. So:

  • Deposit paid → status = 'active'. The spot is theirs, the count goes up, the trip can fill.
  • Onboarding finished → the Plan tab, the chat and the member list appear.

In practice these are seconds apart — deposit is step 4 of 8. But when a trip is nearly full, that ordering is the difference between “sorry, full” before paying and a refund after.

The operator now needs two numbers dashboard

Approving no longer fills the boat, so the operator will over-approve — that is the whole point. The dashboard must stop saying “8 of 10” and start saying “6 confirmed · 5 still onboarding · 10 spots”. Otherwise they cannot tell whether the trip is full or empty.

The one thing missing small add

Today the public trip page has no direct “Message the organiser” button. Getting there means tapping the organiser → profile → message. If DM is the escape hatch for a traveler who cannot pay, it should be one tap from the trip page. That is the Message pill drawn on the organiser row in 0b.
